\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper we measured how much accuracy weight-only HQQ quantization removes from \V{nDatasets} fine-grained datasets, three backbones and three checkpoint regimes.
Bit width and group size set the level of retention, and the datasets separate only in the transition band, where the soybean and cotton cultivar datasets consistently lose more.
Small baseline margins accompany the loss, and matching them narrows the gap without closing it.
Baseline accuracy predicts much of the difficulty, but datasets with equal baseline accuracy still differ in retention by up to \V{pairsMaxDiff}, and the margin stays associated with the difficulty once accuracy is controlled.
Each dataset is tied to its trained checkpoints, with one checkpoint and one inference seed per context, so these differences are associations rather than effects of the data alone.
