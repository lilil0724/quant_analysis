\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper we asked whether the dataset affects the robustness of weight-only HQQ quantization, and how.
Over \V{nDatasets} fine-grained datasets, three backbones and three fine-tuning strategies, bit width and group size set the level of \term{accuracy retention}, and the datasets separate in the transition band between collapse and full retention.
In that band, the five UFG cultivar datasets lose more than the FG datasets in every setting.
The separation of the two groups is stable across backbones and strategies, while the order within the FG group is not.
The \term{vulnerable-tail margin} of the baseline logits correlates with the difficulty and separates some datasets with equal baseline accuracy.
Matching the margin distributions narrows the \term{retention gap} by a median of \V{reductionMedian}\% without closing it, and most UFG swaps go to classes beyond the baseline's second choice.
The top-1 to top-2 margin is therefore a useful summary of the logits, and margins to lower-ranked classes are the next step toward explaining the rest of the gap.
