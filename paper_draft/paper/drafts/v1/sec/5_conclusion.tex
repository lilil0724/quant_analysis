\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper we measured how much accuracy weight-only HQQ quantization removes from \V{nDatasets} fine-grained datasets, three backbones and three checkpoint regimes.
Bit width and group size set the level of retention, and the datasets separate only in the transition band between collapse and full retention.
In that band, the soybean and cotton cultivar datasets consistently lose more, and the ranking of datasets is stable across quantization conditions but only partly across backbones and regimes.
Small baseline margins accompany the loss, and matching them narrows the gap between the groups without closing it.
Baseline accuracy predicts much of the difficulty, but datasets with equal baseline accuracy still differ in retention by up to \V{pairsMaxDiff}, and the margin stays associated with the difficulty once accuracy is controlled.
